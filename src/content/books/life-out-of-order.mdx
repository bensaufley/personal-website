I loved _The Time Traveler's Wife_. I loved it so much that I even watch the much-maligned movie sometimes, just to revisit that world briefly. I understand people's concerns about the "grooming" aspect of that novel, but I think it's kind of hard to discuss outside of the context of _time travel_ and what this sequel frequently refers to as "causal loops." In fact this book directly (if shortly) addresses the uncomfortable nature of that aspect of the first book.

But this isn't a review of that novel. This is a review of the sequel.

I really liked it! It's a very, very different book: where the first one was often cozy, this one feels a lot more stressful, with the looming shadow of climate change, COVID, and worldwide rising authoritarianism – it's much less of an escape than its predecessor. But I think that's good. If I wanted that story, I'd re-read that story (or, as I said, watch the movie). By my recollection this book does feel a lot more _out of order_ than the previous one: it definitely keeps a kind of linear progression, but because of the way characters jump around through time, it can feel very disorganized and non-linear. The kind of fatalist struggle with causal loops is I think maybe a bit more directly addressed in this one, and the introduction of a more science fiction subject matter, AI, the interstices, and the future as a whole, lent it a very different feel.

I was a little unsatisfied with the third act, <Spoiler>which early portions really seemed to indicate were building to something big, maybe something very sci-fi, but in the end, the meaning was much more personal than I expected. I was hoping that what Alba was setting up in the first act was going to pay off as something more global, maybe more hopeful after a near-future of dread, cruelty, and chaos in the world. But it was mostly a setup to resolve her own life, and the lives of those important to her.</Spoiler>Much like the first book, the ending had kind of happened before the end, so the actual ending felt a bit slower, softer. I didn't mind it, because, well, it had already happened. But it wasn't exactly the ending I was hoping to read about.
