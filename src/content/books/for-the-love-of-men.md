This book is really, really good. I mostly read fiction, so my nonfiction pile grows and languishes, but this one grabbed me and I found myself going to it instead of my fiction books.

Some of the political stuff, some of the coverage of toxic masculinity as a whole, was definitely not new to me. But this book is full of science, studies, data that was mostly new to me. Fascinating stuff about testosterone, studies about men's behaviors (regardless of professed ideologies), and a lot of stuff that made me think a lot about my own relationship to masculinity; the way I, despite rejecting toxic masculinity and never really being a part of groups that tend to value that type of behavior, still find myself shaped in so many ways by the invisible expectations of a society that's obsessed with it.

I can't recommend this one enough. I want every man I know to read it. I will be recommending this book for years.

I think it's also worth noting: while obviously as a white cis straight man, I have little room to throw stones, I'm also well aware of how white feminism can overlook so many issues, and even reinforce prejudices. Plank, by my evaluation, did a really good job approaching this from an intersectional standpoint, spending a decent amount of time on the ways gender and race overlap, how the trans experience differs, etc.

I also think that while I'm sure there are red-pilled dudes out there that would be triggered by every page in this book, it's really approachable. It's not "scolding" anybody, as the cliché goes. It's really investigating, presenting evidence, and provoking introspection.
