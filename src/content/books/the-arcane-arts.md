I don't know about this one. It was _much hornier_ than I expected it to be. I don't read books for "spice,"[^1] and this one spent far too much time in the middle third on that for _my_ enjoyment; but more than that, the backdrop of this book is the brutal murder of a woman (and another, less-explained murder of three people) and it felt really uncomfortable to me to be focusing so much on sex with that in mind. Beyond that, BDSM bordering on CNC—perhaps especially with that aforementioned backdrop—is also very much _not my thing_, and that's before we get to the (explicitly acknowledge) power imbalance. I think the authors did OK with the sticky issues at play there, but it still wasn't something I enjoyed.

The underlying sans-spice plot was entertaining and it was compellingly written. But man, there was a lot of stuff that made this book tough for me, and I just powered through it to get to the next book on my shelf.

[^1]: I don't read books for "spice" but I try to read "spicy" books occasionally because a) it's a genre that's generally written off as "for women" and that's dumb and misogynist and it's worth reading things that are not marketed to oneself b) sometimes they have really compelling plots _around_ the "spice." And I guess c) sometimes the right amount of spice is still entertaining, even if it's not something I would seek out. I really enjoyed Danielle Jensen's _Saga of the Unfated_ duology, for example.
