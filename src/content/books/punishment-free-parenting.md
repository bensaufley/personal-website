It was kind of surreal to transition from _Are Prisons Obsolete?_ to this one and discover _just how much_ they flow together. The gist of both, really, is "punishment does not correct behavior." He does a good job explaining how we treat a number of words as synonymous—consequence, discipline, punishment—when they're not. Basically a bunch of these words have become _euphemisms_ for punishment when they were originally intended as more of what they were, and that applies to prisons and to children.

Some of the history of parenting advice was honestly shocking, even though I've encountered some of it before. The things parents of recently-prior generations were told was at times _cartoonishly_ cruel.

But I think this book does a really good job of addressing the criticisms that the title might induce: "punishment-free" does not mean no discipline, no boundaries, etc. It means finding the best ways to set those boundaries in ways kids will understand and respect them. I think it's not a perfect book for a number of reasons but I do think there was a lot in here that was worth reading, as a parent and honestly as a citizen.
